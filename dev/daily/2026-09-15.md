### Why two agents and not three

Three is the documented ceiling in `.claude/rules/container-capacity-scheduling.md`,
but that rule caps on **RAM**, and yesterday's run measured a second, tighter
ceiling it does not mention: **each agent worktree costs ~16 GB once it runs
`dune build`** (`H-AGENT-WORKTREE-DISK-16GB-EACH`). Disk at run start was **62 GB
free of 145 GB (58% used)**. Three agents want ~48 GB of that.

Worse, the two limits interact perversely: the one-dune mutex **serializes** the
expensive part, so a third agent buys **no parallelism** while multiplying disk
cost linearly. Yesterday's measurement caught exactly this — agent 1 had a 16 GB
`_build` while agents 2 and 3 sat queued on the mutex having done no `dune` work
at all. Two agents is the throughput-optimal point on this runner, not a
concession to caution.
