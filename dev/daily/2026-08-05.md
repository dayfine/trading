### The two findings worth more than the merges

**1. A recorded blocker was inverted, and nobody had noticed.** `dev/status/support-floor-stops.md`
and `stop_types.mli` both said the panel path could not honour `split_safe_floors` because
"`daily_view` exposes raw highs/lows + adjusted closes but **no raw close**". The opposite was
true: `daily_view.closes` was populated from `Snapshot_schema.Close` (**raw**) while its
`panel_views.mli` docstring claimed *"Daily adjusted closes."* The missing half was the
**adjusted** close. The writer renamed `closes` → `raw_closes` and added `adjusted_closes` rather
than adding a sibling field, on the argument that a field whose name and docstring disagree with
its value will silently change meaning under any future read — the rename forces the compiler to
surface every site, which is how two mis-commented readers in `trade_audit_report_bin.ml` were
found.

This matters beyond the fix: the flag was **silently inert on the main multi-symbol strategy
path**. That is an `experiment-flag-discipline.md` **R2 defect**, not merely an incomplete
feature — the flag is exposed as the searchable axis `((stops_config ((split_safe_floors true))))`,
so any walk-forward surface run over it was measuring the bar-list half only and would have
under-stated the mechanism. qc-behavioral confirmed this by building merge base `04e2c75b` in a
second worktree: on main, flag-on == flag-off in **every** row.

**2. I gave a wrong instruction and the writer refused it, with numbers.** On finding B2 I
prescribed degrading an unusable adjusted close to factor 1.0 **per bar**, described as "=
flag-off behaviour for that bar". That parenthetical was the error: at bar granularity it
produces a window that is adjusted everywhere *except* one bar. The writer measured before
implementing and found the un-rescaled bar's high (412) out-tops every adjusted high (110) and
becomes the anchor — i.e. **per-bar degradation creates exactly the mixed-basis window that the
other finding was about.** It implemented whole-window instead and argued the invariant: *the
scanned window is always on exactly one price basis*, so the flag returns either the correct
adjusted answer or the flag-off answer, never a third one.

qc-behavioral then retracted its own prescription in writing — *"The writer is right and I was
wrong… my fix for B2 would have re-created B1"* — and endorsed whole-window on the merits.
qc-structural separately verified the invariant is **code-enforced, not fixture-dependent**: the
all-or-nothing decision is taken once, before any rescaling, and no path can produce a partially
rescaled bundle.

The writer also sharpened the argument in a way I want recorded, because it generalises: under
whole-window, a legacy warehouse lacking the `Adjusted_close` column yields results
**bit-identical to baseline**, which is *diagnosable* — `on == off` is precisely the signature
used to prove inertness. NaN-propagation instead produced different-but-wrong numbers (99.0,
106.08) that would enter a walk-forward ledger looking like a **genuine negative result**. A
failure mode indistinguishable from a legitimate REJECT is the most expensive kind in an
experiment programme.

### harness [MERGED #2211]

- **H-AUDIT-MODE-ORDER-UNPINNED closed — but iteration 0's fix was itself incomplete, and the
  reason generalises.** The first attempt added a `WRITE_AUDIT_TEST_ABORT_AFTER_RENAME` hook as
  the literal next statement after `mv -f`. qc-behavioral showed that is **structurally blind**:
  anything a refactor inserts *between* the `mv` and the hook runs to completion before the hook

  source-order check** reading `write_audit.sh` directly, with whole-line comments blanked first

  Asserts (i) the `chmod 644 "$TMP_FILE"` line precedes the `mv` line, and (ii) **zero**
  occurrences of any `chmod` targeting `$OUTPUT_FILE` anywhere in the file.

  better reason than the writeup gave.** B2's regex is spelling-specific and was evaded three ways

  a coverage count.
