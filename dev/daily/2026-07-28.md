### The #2143 finding worth reading

The PR's whole purpose is that a green verification result can be trusted. The reviewer
held it to exactly that standard and found it wanting — not in the fix, which survived
three independent mutations, but in the **wiring of its own regression check**:

| run | `dev/lib/run-in-env.sh` | `_build` | `dune runtest devtools/checks/` |
|---|---|---|---|
| rt1 | fixed (PR tip) | cold | exit 0 — check ran, 5/5 OK printed |
| rt2 | **reverted to the pre-fix buggy version** | warm | exit 0 — **output: 0 lines. Never executed.** |

`ci.yml` restores `trading/_build` keyed on `hashFiles('trading/**/…')`; `dev/lib/**` is
not in that key and there is deliberately no `restore-keys` fallback — so a `dev/lib`-only
change produces an *exact* cache hit, and plain `dune runtest` (no `--force`) skips the
rule. A future PR regressing `run-in-env.sh` alone would have gone green, and this check
is the only defense — `ci.yml` never invokes `run-in-env.sh` itself (grep count 0).

The reviewer explicitly weighed letting it go as a follow-up: the wiring is a
**pre-existing systemic pattern** across ~25 check scripts, and the dune comment's cited
precedent was accurate. What decided it: for the other 24, a stale skip means a doc
convention goes unchecked for a cycle; for this one it means *the guard on whether build
verification can be trusted* silently stops guarding. That is the right call, and it is
the distinction I would want applied again.

After the rework, the same experiment returns **exit 1, 5 of 6 assertions RED**, and a
new rt0b step proves the rule re-runs on an already-warm build rather than serving a
cached PASS.

### The #2145 finding worth reading — a correct claim with a wrong stated reason

The PR's load-bearing claim is **R1 bit-identity**: "with an OLD-hash warehouse (or any
split-free data, factor 1.0) behavior is bit-identical to main." Get this wrong and every
prebuilt golden and historical backtest comparison in the repo is silently invalidated.

Structural rated it HIGH confidence from four inspection points. The behavioral reviewer
probed it **bitwise** with `Int64.bits_of_float` instead, and the result is more
interesting than either a pass or a fail:

- **The mechanism is bitwise exact, not merely close.** IEEE-754 `x /. x` is exactly
  `1.0` and `h *. 1.0` is exactly `h`, verified across 7 magnitudes (1e-8 … 1e9). The
  `0.9999999999999999` failure mode I asked them to look for does not materialise.
- **But the stated precondition is wrong in the direction that matters.** The real
  condition is `adjusted_close = close_price`, not "split-free". With a dividend-only
  adjustment (close 100.0, adjusted 99.87) bit-identity is **false** — and real EODHD
  data has `adjusted_close ≠ close_price` on essentially every historical bar of every
  dividend payer, while `compute_windowed`/`of_daily_bars` rescale **unconditionally**.
- **Every fixture in the affected suites sets `adjusted_close = close_price`**, so the
  suite is structurally incapable of exposing the gap. This is the same shape as the
  `Float.abs` finding on #2139 last run: a headline contract that holds only for the data
  the suite happens to generate.
- R1's operative conclusion still holds — but because of the `Raw` default plus
  non-recomputation of prebuilt artifacts, **not** because the rescale is a no-op.
  Structural reached the right answer through a misreading.

Two of eight mutations survived and are the blocking findings: the documented corrupt/NaN
