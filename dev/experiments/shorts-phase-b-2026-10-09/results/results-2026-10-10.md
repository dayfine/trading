**Which bucket carries the loss, and does a book rule address it** (README item 4):

- **The loss: failed breakdowns.** These are the shorts whose stop never moved: −$758k (v0) and −$713k (v1). Every other
  exit together nets +$148k and +$166k.
  - The failure is visible before any stop acts: over 8 weeks the selected stocks rise more often than they fall.
  - No entry cohort is positive on mean and median in both arms, whether defined by book criteria or by timing. That
    includes: the index in Stage 4 (book §6.1 item 1; those entries do *worse* per trade, −4.7 %, than with the index in
    a Stage 1 base, −2.9 %); a big prior advance (Ch. 7, "has taken off like a rocket"; ≥ 100 %: −4.6 / −7.0 %); RS
    negative and declining (+$5k in v0, −$59k in v1, so the sign flips); the "ideal" combination of weak group, clean
    support, Stage-3→4 and RS topped (49 and 47 trades, mean −5.0 %); SPY drawdown or momentum at entry; and a ticket
    priced above the decision close. Per-split numbers are in `anatomy.sh`.
  - **Book rules that would address it:** better candidate selection (Ch. 7, "How to do it right", "Taking some
    profitable steps"). As proxied here, those rules do not separate winners from losers.
- **The squeeze sub-bucket**, about −$200k a cell. The book's tool is the "sucker short" rule (short interest ≥ 5 ×
  average daily volume, Ch. 7). The warehouse has no short-interest data, and gap-ups go through any stop.
- **The gain: long holds with MFE ≥ 20 %**, which gave back $743k and $701k from their peaks.
  - The book's tools are the trader's buy-stops, partial covering at the swing-rule target or a downsloping trendline,
    and covering above the 30-week MA (Ch. 7, "Using buy-stops the trader's way", "Trendlines on the downside").
  - They are bounded. Perfect hindsight at every winner's peak would add at most $743k / $701k against TR P&L of
    −$730k / −$656k, leaving the 26-year book at about +$13k (v0 s0) and +$45k (v1 s0) ex interest: +1.3 % and
    +4.5 %. That is before any path effects, and B is untested.
  - The realistic tool on the loss side, the pack's static hard-stop replay, recovers +$370k / +$252k at 3 %. It also
    cuts 20 and 19 eventual winners and leaves realised P&L at −$240k / −$294k before about $110–120k of costs.
- **Authorities.** Tier 1 is `docs/design/weinstein-book-reference.md` §6.1 (the short entry checklist: market in
  Stage 4, group negative, prior advance into a Stage 3 top, breakdown below support and the MA, RS topped, little
  support below), §6.2 (the trader's full position on the breakdown; volume not required) and §6.3 (the buy-stop above
  the prior rally peak, trailed after failed rallies, and "use downsloping trendlines for partial profit-taking"). Tier
  2 is the book, Ch. 7, read locally for this question and cited by section name only.
- **Decision 10's round-2 condition is not met (my reading).** Anatomy C names where the loss is, but not a specific
  cause that a book rule fixes:
  - the selection rules that address failed breakdowns do not separate them in the data;
  - the squeeze rule needs data the warehouse lacks;
  - the exit-side rules cannot, even at their hindsight bound, take the book meaningfully above zero.

  §8.6 gives what a round 2 would have to be if the user reads the condition differently.

### 8. Reading

#### 8.1 Verdict, calibrated

**What this design can claim.**
- The short-only Weinstein book as specified fails the pre-registered stopping rule in round 1, and the margin is not
  close. That holds for v0 (every merged short fix, the faithful Ch. 7 trail) and for v1 (v0 plus stale-ticket
  cancellation) over 26 years on the broad PIT universe with total-return accounting.
- A misses by −0.79 to −0.94 log. Only 2008–09 of the three bears is positive.
- This is the real test: the mechanism is built, backtested on the full window and paired by salt. It is not a proxy
  screen.

**What it cannot claim.**
- That no single-name short book can work under different entry rules, universes or data. A short-interest-aware
  squeeze filter or the full Ch. 7 trader preset was not tested.
- That shorts have no portfolio value next to the long book. Decision 8 asks whether the short book makes money by
  itself; hedge value is the index-hedge question.
- That the salts replicate. They are one trade path, and the salt spread (≤ 0.011 log) measures fill noise only.
- That v1's flags are better. Their 26-year gain over v0 rests about half on one data-defect trade and reverses in
  two bears.

**Promotion.** None. Both v1 flags stay default-off axes (R1–R3). In Rule 4 terms they are REJECT-as-default but a
legitimate axis, not do-not-revive. They do what they say (0 stale fills), and the defect they happened to avoid
should be fixed in the data instead.

#### 8.2 Why: the result decomposed

1. **Exposure timing, about −0.30 log (v0) and −0.26 (v1) over 26 years.**
   - The book is short only in macro-Bearish weeks. Under the lagged below-a-falling-MA regime those include the
     bear-market rallies and V-rebounds: SPY rose 66.7 % on the 26 years' DOWN-regime days, where the book's exposure
     averaged 25–27 %.
   - An SPY short on the same path earns in deep bears (+0.094 and +0.229 log in the 2000–02 and 2008–09 periods) but
     bleeds in the rebounds and in 2022 (−0.025). Net, it is negative.
   - This is the book's own market rule doing what it says: shorts wait until the averages are "below the 30-week MAs
     and in Stage 4" (Ch. 7, "The Market"). A confirmed-bear gate is late by construction.
2. **The names, about −0.49 log (v0) and −0.41 (v1), negative in every bear period.**
   - Without stops, the selected Stage-4 breakdowns rose on average over 1 to 13 weeks and fell over 8 weeks in only
     34–36 % of cases. Mean reversion after a breakdown beat continuation in this universe.
   - They also underperformed an index short over the same bars by −2.0 to −2.8 % per 8 weeks.
   - The payoff shape is the long book's mirror image. The right tail is capped (best +63.7 %, winners +19 % on
     average), while the left tail is fat: squeezes −16 % to −28 %, takeover gaps, rally days.
   - An 18 % hit rate at a 1.93 payoff loses about 4.7 % a trade. The long book's edge is its uncapped right tail
     (`project_edge_is_the_fat_tail`), and a short book cannot have one.
3. **Stops and exits: they reshape the loss, they do not cause it.**
   - The initial stop is where the failed breakdowns end: 53–56 % of trades, −10.4 % each, all losers.
   - The trail gives back most of the winners' open gains ($0.70–0.74M).
   - Both are bounded (§4): no realistic exit rule closes a −$0.66M to −$0.73M TR gap.
   - The winners' MAE (22 of 35 beyond 4 %) and the whipsaws (41 D/F) pull the stop width in opposite directions:
     there is no free width.
4. **Costs, about −0.14 log (v0) and −0.13 (v1).**
   - Borrow is the largest part: $75.7k in v0 s0, of which **88 % accrued on the 1,189 position-days marked between $5
     and $17**, at the spec's 25 %/yr hard-to-borrow tier. That tier taxes exactly the shorts that work. NKTR alone paid
     $4.1k, DB $2.7k.
   - Then dividends (0.045 log) and commissions ($8.6k).
   - Removing all costs leaves A at about −0.79 (v0) and −0.66 (v1).
5. **Data and simulator defects: real, small next to the gap** (§8.7).
   - HRC −$52.7k (v0 only).
   - Late fills on breaker-budget days −$33k and −$39k.
   - The NKTR liquidity exit, +$62k, is a #3136 mixed-basis artefact that *helped* v0.
   - The stuck Momentum vote changed 38–39 pre-2017 tickets, which netted −$50k (v0) and +$1k (v1).
   - Correcting all of them moves A by well under 0.1 log.

Squeezes and forced covers were also checked, and they are part of item 2's left tail (about −$200k a cell, a third
of the realised loss) rather than a separate cause.

#### 8.4 Perturbation, before believing a split

- **No positive cohort survives both arms.** RS "Negative_declining" is −1.6 % (v0, +$5k) but −5.7 % (v1, −$59k).
  `weeks_declining = 3` is +0.3 % in v1 and −1.6 % in v0. The "ideal" book setup is −5.0 % in both.
- **The selection read holds at every horizon.** 5, 20, 40 and 65 bars; both arms; all salts identical.
- **The index-stage split runs against the book's market rule.** Entries with the index in Stage 4 do worse per trade
  than with the index in a Stage 1 base, in both arms.
- **The v1 − v0 gain halves without HRC** (+$64k → about +$11k) and is negative in two bears. Its sign at 26 years
  rests on one defect.
- **Late fills against the breaker budget.** 14 of 14 and 13 of 13 late exits had a trigger-day close more than 15 %
  beyond the fill, against 1–2 of 178 and 1 of 152 same-day exits. The exceptions are EME 2008-12-08 in every cell
  and FHCC 2003-03-17 in v0 s1. The association is strong but not deterministic, so the root cause is left to the
  simulator issue.

#### 8.5 How it fits what we know

- **Confirms and extends `project_shorts_liveness_dead` (Phase A).**
  - Phase A's clean remainder was about −22 % / −17 % over 2007–12 (price-only, v11, salt 0). With every fix and
    total-return accounting over 26 years, it is −66 % to −73 % ex interest.
  - Phase A's "exit side" mechanisms are refined. The Tightened freeze is fixed, but the Trailing freeze and the give-back
    remain. Squeezes are confirmed and clustered.
  - The new piece is upstream of the exits: the selected names rise before any stop acts.
- **Consistent with P0a** (`project_p0_levers_no_build_2026_06_20`): on the deep broad run a reserved short sleeve lost
  at every fraction. Two different short books on broad universes, the same sign.
- **Refines P1a** (`project_p1a_deep_short_screens`, an sp500 cell, so not evidence under `universe-discipline.md`). Its
  idea was that the value of shorts is hedge-shaped and early-bear. Here, on the book's own exposure path, an *index*
  short earns in the deep bears (+0.094 and +0.229 log) where the names do not (−0.093 and −0.071). If short exposure
  has value, it is index-shaped, which supports routing bear protection to the index-hedge decision.
- **Re-derives the slow-grind ledger note** (`2026-06-22-slow-grind-adlive-wfcv`): "shorts admitted late in a decline
  get squeezed at the 2002/2009 bottoms". The give-back windows lose −15 % (2002–03) and −18 % (2009) at 18–35 %
  exposure.
- **Mirrors `project_edge_is_the_fat_tail` on the other side.** The long book's edge is the uncapped right tail; the
  short book's right tail is capped and its left tail fat, so "let the winners run" cannot carry it.
- **Agrees with `project_cascade_selection_inversion` in spirit.** Screener-score quartiles do not rank the shorts
  (`anatomy.sh`, equal-count quartiles of `screener_score_at_entry`). From Q1 to Q4, P&L is −$79k / −$105k / −$317k /
