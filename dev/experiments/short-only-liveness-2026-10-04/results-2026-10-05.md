## Verdict, calibrated

- **What this design can claim.** Both arms are **live**: the short-only book trades once the slow-grind
  gate is off and tickets are priced at the close. It can also size the defects in this run and give the
  sign of each P&L component in this window at salt 0.
- **What it cannot claim.**
  - Whether a short-only book makes money (rule 4, and one salt and one 5-year window).
  - Any arm comparison (V6 failed).
  - Anything about the 26-year record.
  - The bucket split is an accounting attribution, not a counterfactual. The 60 and 40 sub-$17 tickets
    held notional the run would otherwise have given to other top-N candidates. soT's audit lists 1,509
    alternatives skipped with `Short_notional_cap`, 14 of them on AABA's 2011-06-03 entry alone.
- **Contamination, stated plainly.** Bucket A (#3131) is **+$96.1k soT and +$72.7k soTs gross**, about
  +$77.8k and +$60.3k after the commission and borrow it paid. It is 41 of soT's 62 winners and 27 of
  soTs's 46, and all of each arm's 2008 entry-year profit. Together with CHMT_old (+$55.4k net, soT only), it
  flattered soT's headline by about 13.3 pp and soTs's by about 6.0 pp.

## Why

**P&L split.** Every closed trade falls into one bucket, assigned in the order A → B → C → E:

| component | soT n (wins) | soT P&L | soTs n (wins) | soTs P&L |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|
| A. filled below $17; all margin-called, covered at the next open (#3131) | 60 (41) | +$96.1k | 40 (27) | +$72.7k |
| B. filled ≥ $17, margin-called after the mark fell under $17 (tier step, D7) | 4 (4) | +$31.8k | 6 (6) | +$34.7k |
| C. stale ticket filled in a non-Bearish week (rule 3) | 5 (0) | −$25.8k | 4 (1) | −$17.9k |
| D. CHMT_old, open (splice, D4) | 1 | +$63.2k | – | – |
| E. rest, closed | 106 (17) | −$275.7k | 66 (12) | −$169.2k |
| E. rest, open at end | 5 | +$66.5k | 7 | +$34.5k |
| costs not in trade P&L (commission + borrow) | | −$39.9k | | −$50.1k |
| **total = final NAV − $1M** | | **−$83.9k** | | **−$95.2k** |

The 68 and 50 `margin_call` rows in `trades.csv` are buckets A and B: 64 and 46 maintenance calls, every
one at a mark under $17 according to the audit's `current_price`. The remaining 4 in each arm are forced
covers of losing shorts at prices above $17: ALX, SUN1, SSC and AET in soT; JPM, UNH, MCK and BFAM_old in
soTs (D6).

- **A, the churn.** The $17 gate passes a candidate on its base-top `suggested_entry`, but the ticket is
  priced at the decision close. Once filled below $17, a short opens at an equity ratio of 0.50 against a
  0.83 maintenance tier (1.00 under $5). It is flagged at the first close unless it has already fallen
  about 18 %, and it is covered at the next open. The P&L is one overnight move, mostly in the 2008–09
  crash: mean +1.6 % and median +0.9 % per trade (soT; p10 −3.2 %, p90 +6.9 %), median hold 1 day,
  maximum 4. It is not an annualisable edge.
- **E, the rest: the book the rules intend.** soT made −$209.2k gross, −22.2 % of starting capital after
  its estimated costs. soTs made −$134.7k gross and −17.1 % after costs.
  - Win rate is 16 % (17/106) and 18 % (12/66).
  - Trade return: soT median −4.6 % (p10 −11.1 %, p25 −6.1 %, p75 −3.4 %, p90 +5.7 %). soTs median −6.1 %
    (p10 −14.6 %, p75 −2.9 %, p90 +15.7 %).
  - Every entry year is negative except soTs 2011 (+$6.4k).
  - B would survive the #3131 fix, since those trades filled at $17 or more. Counting B as clean gives
    −$177.4k and −$99.9k gross.

**Why the rest loses.** Two mechanisms are visible. The data say little either way about entry
selection.

1. **The short book does not harvest its winners: the fat-tail tax, on the short side.** At each arm's
   NAV peak, the open shorts carried large gains that those positions never realised:

   | arm | NAV peak | open shorts at the peak | open gain | finally realised |
   |---|---|---|---:|---:|
   | soT | 2008-10-27 | SHAW, FWRD, FCX, ETN, ERIE, BJRI | +$211.7k | +$97.9k |
   | soTs | 2009-03-09 (the SPY low) | UAL, TPL, TECK, SKYW, BAC, ADSK, CINF | +$304.7k | +$41.6k |

   soTs's CINF went from +$52.0k open to +$6.9k open at the end. Among rest trades whose favourable
   excursion reached ≥ 20 %, mean MFE was 37.8 % against a realised 9.9 % (soT, n = 23). For soTs it was
   44.8 % against 10.5 % (n = 22, including ANG's zero-bar artifact, D11).

   The short Tightened state cannot lower its stop (D2). ADSK, TPL and CINF logged 137, 130 and 222
   `Tightened_hold` decisions. TECK's stop sat at 31.21 from 2008-10-01 to 2009-04-20 while the stock
   fell to 2.67 (2009-03-05). The max drawdowns start at those peaks.
2. **Squeezes run through the stops on bear-market rally days.** soT's four largest losers and three of
   soTs's four are forced covers at −15 % to −31 %, against planned stops of 4–15 %. They came on rally
   days such as the 2008-11-24 bailout rally and April 2009. 21 of 106 and 11 of 64 stop exits filled
   more than 0.5 % above the stop.

**Other mechanisms.**

- **Timing.** Stale tickets cost −$25.8k and −$17.9k. Counting every ticket that rested at least 4 weeks
  widens that to −$41.4k and −$31.7k.
- **Costs and turnover.** Costs were 4.0 % and 5.0 % of capital. The churn alone paid about $18.3k and
  $12.4k of it: commission on low-priced share counts, plus 25–100 %/yr borrow.
- **Gate interaction.** The share-class gate does not apply to shorts (D3). `neutral_blocks_shorts` holds
  at placement but resting tickets bypass it.
- **Data.** CHMT_old (D4) and DRWI (D5).

- *Bucket A's sign holds for any threshold.* At fill < $15, $16, $18 and $19, A is +$90.9k, +$88.7k,
