# Weinstein Rule Card (weekly charts)

Source: Stan Weinstein, *Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets* (1988), read in full from the OCR text. The rules are paraphrased. Citations give the chapter and a short phrase from it.

Strength tags:
- **[E]**: the book states the rule explicitly.
- **[X]**: the book shows it only in a worked example or a chart case.
- **[I]**: my own inference, used to make the book operational.

Price units: the book quotes 1988 fractions. "1/8 above" means one tick above, and "1/4 / 1/2 point" means a small fixed cushion. Wherever I convert these to decimals, the rule is tagged [I].

---

## 0. Data conventions

- **Weekly bar:** the Monday–Friday high, low and close, plus that week's volume. [E] (Ch 1, "plot the high, low, and close for the entire week")
- **30-week MA:** this Friday's close plus the prior 29 Friday closes, divided by 30. [E] (Ch 1, "closing price for this Friday night added to the prior 29")
  - Mansfield charts use a *weighted* 30-week MA. It turns faster and gives a few more whipsaws. [E] (Ch 1)
  - I will read whatever MA the chart shows and treat the two as equivalent. [I]
- **Relative strength (RS):** stock price ÷ market average, computed weekly on the same day, preferably Friday. [E] (Ch 4, "My preference is each Friday")
  - The Mansfield RS has a zero line. Above zero is a long-term positive, below zero a long-term negative. [E] (Ch 4)
  - The trend of the RS line matters more than its level. [E] (Ch 4, "overall pattern is the most important factor")
- **Investor horizon:** up to 12 months. **Trader horizon:** catches each 2–4 month move. [E] (Ch 1)
- **Analysis cadence:** do the analysis at the weekend, about one hour, away from intraday noise. [E] (Ch 3, "set time aside each weekend")

## 1. Stage definitions and transitions

| Stage | Weekly chart signature | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Base** | Follows a decline of several months. The MA loses its downside slope and flattens. Price whips above and below the MA and swings between support and resistance. Can last months or years. [E] (Ch 2, "30-week MA loses its downside slope") | Usually dries up. It may expand late in Stage 1 while price stays flat, which is favorable. [E] (Ch 2) |
| **2 Advance** | A breakout above the top of resistance *and* above the 30-week MA. The MA turns up shortly afterwards. Successive higher peaks and higher lows. All corrections hold above the rising MA. [E] (Ch 2, "each successive rally peak is higher") | Impressive on the breakout. Expands on rallies and contracts on dips. [E] (Ch 1–2) |
| **3 Top** | The MA loses its upward slope and flattens. Price "tiptoes" below and above the MA. Sharp, choppy, sideways moves. [E] (Ch 2, "tiptoe below and above the MA") | Usually heavy ("churning"). [E] (Ch 2) |
| **4 Decline** | A break below the bottom of the support zone. Price sits below a declining MA. Lower lows, and lower peaks on rallies. The old support becomes the new ceiling. [E] (Ch 2) | A volume increase is *not* required. [E] (Ch 2, "doesn't necessarily need such a huge increase") |

Transition rules:
- **4→1:** the decline loses momentum, price goes sideways and the MA stops falling. Do not buy yet: your money is tied up and the base can fail. [E] (Ch 2)
- **1→2:** a weekly breakout above the top of the base resistance while price is above an MA that is *no longer declining*. [E] (Ch 1, "MA, which must no longer be declining")
  - A breakout from a base that happens *below* the MA, or while the MA is still declining, is **not** Stage 2. [E] (Ch 3, Western Union and Cannon Group cases)
- **Late Stage 2 is "hold, not buy":** the MA's angle of ascent slows a lot, and price is far above both support and the MA. [E] (Ch 2, "overextended and most definitely no longer a buy")
- **2→3:** the MA flattens after a big advance and price starts crossing the MA. [E] (Ch 2 quiz answers 5 and 9)
- **A Stage 3 that is still above major support is not yet Stage 4.** [E] (Ch 2 quiz answer 11)
- **3→4:** a break below the support zone at the bottom of the top. [X] (Ch 2, ICH breaking 24)
  - The book often pairs this with a break of the MA and of a significant trendline. [E] (Ch 2, Teleprompter)
- **Failed top:** if a potential Stage 3 range breaks out *upward* on very heavy volume, the stock goes back to Stage 2. This is rare and very powerful, and suits traders. [E/X] (Ch 2, Micropolis)
- **Typical paths:**
  - After a breakdown there are almost always one or more rallies back toward the breakdown level before the decline gets going in earnest. [E] (Ch 2)
  - After an initial breakout there is usually at least one pullback toward the breakout point. [E] (Ch 2)
- **Supporting definitions:** [E] (Ch 1)
  - Support and resistance are *zones*, not exact prices.
  - The more often a zone is tested, and the longer the testing lasts, the more significant its eventual break.
  - A significant trendline has at least 3 touches.
  - The flatter the trendline, the more meaningful its break. The steeper it is, the less meaningful.
  - The strongest signal is a significant trendline broken with the long-term MA also crossed "within a matter of days". [E] (Ch 1)
- **Long-range background (10-year yearly high/low bars):**
  - A breakout to a multi-year high means no overhead sellers.
  - A breakdown to a multi-year low means no support below.
  - [E] (Ch 1)

## 2. Market gate

### 2a. What the book uses

- The book applies stage analysis to the **Dow Jones Industrials** with a 30-week MA. It calls this the one indicator to keep if you keep only one. [E] (Ch 8, "If you keep up just one long term indicator")
- I will apply the identical rules to the **S&P 500 chart I have**. [I]
  - The book uses the S&P composite as the RS denominator and says any average will do if used consistently. [E] (Ch 4)

### 2b. Index stage rules (weekly)

1. **Index in Stage 3** (MA flattening after an advance, a toppy pattern of lower highs): proceed with caution and become selective. [E] (Ch 8)
2. **Index breaks below its 30-week MA into Stage 4:** [E] (Ch 8, "Suspend buying even if you see a few stocks")
   - suspend new buying, even when individual stocks break out;
   - sell longs that show poor RS;
   - pull remaining stops up as tight as possible;
   - start hunting for shorts.
3. **While the index MA is declining, stay defensive until the MA levels out.** A temporary pop above the MA does not count while the MA still points lower. [E] (Ch 8, "not enough ... to temporarily pop above")
4. **A dip below a still-rising MA, without breaking support, is not a sell signal.** [X] (Ch 8 footnote, late-1986 Dow)
5. **Bull signal:** after a major decline, the index forms a Stage 1 base and then crosses its *level* 30-week MA upward. Start buying aggressively. [E] (Ch 8, "level 30-week MA is penetrated")
6. **Market trend negative:** do very little buying, if any, even when some stocks break out. [E] (Ch 3, "Forest to the Trees" step 1)
7. **Shorts need all of these:** [E] (Ch 7, "all of the market averages are below")
   - the averages are below their 30-week MAs and in Stage 4;
   - a majority of the long-term gauges are negative.

   A short in a bull market is the exception. Use it only for an outstandingly bearish chart, as a hedge. [E] (Ch 7)
8. **Long in a bear market:** only an A+ pattern, as a hedge against shorts. [E] (Ch 5, National Semiconductor)
9. **Degree of investment:** you do not have to be 100% invested. Be fully invested only when the indicators and charts strongly agree. [E] (Ch 10, "Don't feel you have to be 100 percent")

### 2c. NYSE advance-decline line

- **How to build it:** a cumulative daily net of advances minus declines (unchanged issues ignored), plotted under the Dow. [E] (Ch 8)
- **Confirming:** the A-D line and the index rising together means a significant correction is unlikely. [E] (Ch 8)
- **Negative divergence (tops):** the index makes new highs and the A-D line fails to. [E] (Ch 8)
  - A divergence lasting a few weeks points to a correction inside the bull.
  - A divergence lasting several months is a problem.
  - Historically the A-D line peaks **5 to 10 months before** the blue chips.
  - Each unconfirmed new index high is a "strike".
  - Final confirmation is the index breaking support and its 30-week MA. [X] (Ch 8: 1961, 1965, 1972, 1987 cases)
- **Positive divergence (bottoms):** the index holds above its prior low while the A-D line keeps making new lows. Bullish. [E] (Ch 8, 1957, 1962, 1970, 1984)
- **Duration:** the longer a divergence lasts, the bigger the reversal. [E] (Ch 8)
- **Momentum index:** the 200-day average of daily NYSE net advances. [E] (Ch 8)
  - Interpretation:
    - a zero-line cross is the main signal;
    - a cross after a long, deep stay on one side is more meaningful;
    - it is usually early at tops (peaks before the Dow) and late at bottoms, where it acts as confirmation.
  - I can compute it only if the pack gives daily net advances. If it gives only a cumulative line, I will read the line's slope over about 40 weeks as a proxy. [I]

### 2d. Book indicators I will NOT have

The book itself says no single gauge is used alone; the call is the majority "Weight of the Evidence". [E] (Ch 8)

1. Weekly new highs minus new lows (common stocks only).
2. World markets and Barron's world index versus its 30-week MA.
3. General Motors as bellwether (stage analysis, four-month rule, divergence).
4. Dow price/dividend ratio:
   - 14–17 is a bargain zone;
   - above 26 is dangerous;
   - above 30 is extremely overvalued.

   It is a potential, not a timing signal.
5. Contrary opinion: headlines, magazine covers, sentiment surveys.
6. The percentage of NYSE stocks (or S&P groups) in Stages 1 and 2 (Ch 3 footnote).
7. Short-interest ratio for individual stocks.
8. Presidential cycle and seasonality (calendar-derivable, but fine-tuning only):
   - year 1 of the cycle is usually bearish;
   - year 3 is the best;
   - Nov–Jan is the strongest stretch;
   - Feb, May, Jun and Sep are weak.

   The book says a valid breakout is still bought in a weak month. [E] (Ch 3)
9. A-D lines for the Amex, OTC and Dow, and short-term oscillators.

**My market rule:** I will decide with the three gauges I have: the S&P stage, A-D confirmation or divergence, and sector breadth. [I]

| My state | Required gauges | What I do |
|---|---|---|
| **Bullish** | S&P in Stage 2 (above a rising MA) and the A-D line not in a multi-month negative divergence | Buy normally. |
| **Caution** | S&P in Stage 3, or a multi-month A-D divergence | Buy only A+ setups, tighten stops, take no continuation entries unless they are A+. |
| **Bearish** | S&P below its MA with the MA flat or falling (Stage 4) | No new longs except an A+ hedge; shorts allowed. |
| **Bottoming** | S&P base, plus a positive A-D divergence | Cover shorts and prepare buy lists. Buy once the S&P crosses a level MA. |

## 3. Sector / group gate

- **Top-down order:** market, then group, then stock. Group analysis is "just as important as overall market timing". [E] (Ch 3)
- **Use the same stage criteria on the group chart** (S&P industry charts, which show no volume). [E] (Ch 3)
- **Long requirement:** the group is healthy, meaning **not in Stage 3 or 4**. [E] (Ch 3, "the sector be healthy (That is, not in Stages 3 or 4)")
  - Never buy any stock in a Stage 4 group, however bullish the stock looks. [E] (Ch 3, electric utilities)
  - "Don't buy a stock in a negative group" is one of the Don't Commandments. [E] (Ch 4)
- **Best long pairings:** [E] (Ch 3)
  - Investor: an early-Stage-2 stock in an early-Stage-2 group.
  - Trader: a continuation breakout in a dynamic Stage 2 group.
  - Allowed: a stock breaking out of a first base inside a group already well into Stage 2.
  - Allowed: a continuation buy inside a group that has just entered Stage 2.
- **Group quality boosters:** [E/X] (Ch 3 casino, mobile homes, computers cases)
  - the group's RS line is bullish, moves into positive territory or makes a new high;
  - the group breaks out of a Stage 1 base or completes a consolidation near its MA;
  - the group makes a new all-time high on the long-range chart;
  - *several stocks in the same group* turn bullish at once, which is the strongest cross-check. The group with the most excellent stock charts is the A+ group.
  - Group overhead resistance and group RS grade the group the same way they grade a stock. [E] (Ch 3 quiz answer 12)
- **Cross-check:** a group chart can be distorted by one stock (a takeover or special situation). Confirm against the individual charts. [E] (Ch 3)
- **Short requirement:** [E] (Ch 7, "The Group")
  - the group has broken below its 30-week MA;
  - the group RS is trending lower;
  - several of its stocks act weak.
- **Never short a stock in a Stage 2 or strong group.** If you own a stock in a strong group that breaks down, still sell it, but don't short it. [E] (Ch 3 footnote; Ch 7, Alcoa)
- **Diversification:** spread buys across 3–4 strong groups, not one. [E] (Ch 4)

## 4. Long entry

### 4a. Investor entry (early Stage 2)

**Setup:** a Stage 1 base, with price near a 30-week MA that has stopped declining and has flattened or started to turn up. [E] (Ch 3)

**Breakout level:** just above the top of the resistance zone, for example a 12 top giving a 12 1/8 breakout. [E] (Ch 1)
- My decimal version: the zone top plus one tick, about $0.01 for liquid stocks. [I]

**Required at the breakout week:**
1. **Price** closes above resistance and above the 30-week MA. The MA is not declining; rising is better. [E]
   - The book's trigger is the resting buy-stop being hit. It does not require a weekly close above the level. [E]
   - On a weekly chart I treat a week that trades through the level as a fill. [I]
2. **Volume test (weekly):** at least one of: [E] (Ch 4, "at least twice the average volume of the past month")
   - (a) breakout-week volume is **at least 2× the average of the prior 4 weeks** (preferably more);
   - (b) a **3–4 week build-up of at least 2×** the average of the prior several weeks, plus at least some increase on the breakout week.
   - **Grading tightener:** volume that is double the prior 4 weeks but below other weeks inside the base is only "fair", a negative. Grade A needs breakout volume that tops the base. [X] (Ch 4 quiz answer 9; Ch 5 quiz CSS)
   - **No significant volume increase means don't buy.** [E] (Ch 4, "Never trust a breakout that isn't accompanied")
   - A daily-chart equivalent: the breakout day exceeds 2× the prior week's average. [E] (Ch 4 footnote)
   - Volume shape *inside* the base does not matter. [E] (Ch 4)
3. **RS test:** [E] (Ch 4)
   - Never buy when the RS line is in a downtrend.
   - Never buy when the RS is negative (below zero) *and* stays in poor shape.
   - A below-zero RS is acceptable only when it is clearly improving or trending higher.
   - **RS divergence veto:** if the RS at the breakout is lower than at the prior price peak while price is higher, don't buy. [X] (Ch 4, Acme United, Care, Grand Auto)
   - **Plus:** the RS crossing from negative to positive at the breakout substantially raises the odds. [E] (Ch 1, Ch 4)
4. **Overhead resistance grading:** look for the next resistance above the breakout, including old support that has become resistance. [E] (Ch 4)

   | Grade | Condition |
   |---|---|
   | **A+** | No resistance in the 10-year long-range history, or a move into *virgin territory* (never traded there). |
   | **A** | No resistance visible on the ~2½-year chart. Resistance older than that is "no big deal". |
   | **B / C** | A heavy resistance zone only a few points overhead, for example within about 20% (XYZ: a 15 breakout under an 18–20 zone, graded B−). Repeated yearly highs just above the breakout, as with Pan Am, grade C−. |

   - Older resistance is less potent. Resistance about 18 months to 2 years old can be attacked by a large base with exceptional volume. [E/X] (Ch 4 quiz answer 11; ASA)
   - The ~20% figure is my reading of the XYZ example. [I]
   - "Don't buy a stock that has heavy nearby overhead resistance." [E] (Ch 4 Don'ts)
5. **Base quality:** [E/X] (Ch 4, "Bigger Is Better"; quiz answers 9 and 12)
   - bigger and longer bases give bigger moves; prefer about 12 months or more over about 6;
   - a breakout through a horizontal top beats a breakout through a declining trendline;
   - a longer time spent below resistance makes the breakout more significant (Ch 1).
6. **Reward/risk:** compute the initial stop *before* ordering (see §5).
   - Prefer the candidate with the smallest % risk.
   - Try to limit buys to an initial stop **no more than 15%** below the entry. [E] (Ch 6, "isn't greater than 15 percent")

**Sizing:** buy **half** the position on the breakout and half on the pullback. [E] (Ch 3; Ch 4 checklist, "buy-stop orders for half of your position")

### 4b. Investor pullback add (second half)

Buy the second half when **all** of these hold: [E] (Ch 3–4)
- the stock pulls back close to the breakout price;
- the post-breakout action was good: an impressive volume increase and a move well above the breakout;
- volume contracts on the pullback, for example by more than 75% from the peak (Goodyear). [X]

Further rules:
- **The stock just nudged above resistance and fell back below it:** red flag, no add. [E] (Ch 3)
- **Volume on the pullback stays at peak levels:** do not add. [X] (Ch 4, Goodyear)
- **Frequency:** about **80%** of initial base breakouts pull back; fewer than **50%** of continuation breakouts do. [E] (Ch 3)
- **Breakout fails the volume test:** sell on the first rally. If it fails to rally and falls back below the breakout point, dump it immediately. [E] (Ch 4 checklist)

### 4c. Trader entry (continuation)

**Setup:** [E] (Ch 3, "The Trader's Way")
- the stock is already in Stage 2 with the 30-week MA **clearly rising**;
- it drops back near its MA and consolidates in a new range;
- it then breaks out above the top of that range.

If the MA is rolling over or flattening, don't buy. [E]

**Sizing:** buy the **entire** position on the breakout. [E] (Ch 3, "buy your entire position")
- On an initial base breakout, a trader also buys the whole position. After a fast profit the trader may re-buy on the pullback. [E] (Ch 3 footnote)

**Mix:** [E] (Ch 3, "While there are no official rules")
- Traders do about **80%** continuation buys and 20% early Stage 2 buys.
- Investors do about **75–80%** early Stage 2 buys and 20–25% continuation buys.
- Early in a bull market, investors need no continuation buys at all.
- Late in a bull market, when few first bases remain, investors should do some.

**The same volume, RS, resistance and group tests apply.** [E] (Ch 4 quiz answer 11, National Service)

### 4d. Special patterns

- **Head-and-shoulders bottom:** the most reliable bottom pattern. [E] (Ch 4)
  - Buy when price breaks above the neckline.
  - Also require price above the 30-week MA with the MA no longer declining, and significant volume on the breakout.
  - MA still declining: don't buy. Wait for it to flatten and buy a pullback.
  - Price above the neckline but still below the MA: wait until it clears the MA.
  - Ignore volume inside the formation.
  - Don't anticipate: many of these patterns fail.
- **Double bottom:** valid but common. Be very demanding on all the criteria. [E] (Ch 4)
- **Triple confirmation (investors only):** above the MA plus all three of: [E] (Ch 5)
  - breakout volume above 2× the 4-week average, and larger than anywhere in the base, *followed by several more weeks of heavy volume*;
  - the RS moving from negative or near-zero decisively into positive territory;
  - a pre-breakout advance of roughly **40–50% or more** within the base.

  **Invest much more heavily** in these. [E] (Ch 5, "invest much more heavily in it")
- **Takeover tell:** volume building steadily for about 8 weeks before the breakout. Act only if the chart is a valid buy on its own merits, and use only a small part of capital for rumor plays. [E] (Ch 5)

### 4e. Order type and placement

- **Use a buy-stop just above resistance with a limit, good-'til-canceled.** [E] (Ch 3, "Buy 1,000 XYZ at 12 1/8 stop-12 3/8 limit-GTC")
  - The limit is **¼ point above the breakout** for actively traded stocks.
  - It is **½ point above** for thin stocks.
  - My decimal version: limit = trigger plus about 2% for liquid names, plus about 4% for thin ones. [I]
- **Avoid a plain buy-stop:** it can fill at a gap open far above the level. [E] (Ch 3)
- **Avoid a single-price stop-limit:** it misses about 1 in 4 breakouts. [E] (Ch 3)
- **A GTC order stays in until it fills or I cancel it.** Keep records, and cancel when the pattern changes. [E] (Ch 3 quiz answer 5)
- **Enter orders for the 2–3 best setups found in the weekend scan.** [E] (Ch 3)
- **If the week gaps past my limit, I don't chase.** [I]
  - The book says a little above is fine, but don't pay far above: buy it right or not at all. [E] (Ch 2, "Either buy it right or don't buy it")

### 4f. When NOT to buy (Stan's Don't Commandments)

[E] (Ch 4, Ch 1)
1. The market trend is bearish.
2. The group is negative.
3. Price is below the 30-week MA.
4. The 30-week MA is declining, even with price above it.
5. The stock is too late in its advance, far above the ideal entry.
6. Breakout volume is poor. If a buy-stop filled anyway, sell quickly.
7. RS is poor.
8. Heavy overhead resistance sits nearby.
9. You are guessing a bottom ("cheap after getting smashed").
10. The trigger is good news after a big run, or a low P/E.
11. **Never buy in Stage 3 or 4.** [E] (Ch 10)

**Overextension:** the book gives no number. [I]
- I treat entry as "too late" when price is more than about 15% above the breakout level, or when the stop distance would exceed 15%.
- The book's example is a 12 1/8 breakout: 12 3/8 is fine, 25 is not.

## 5. Long stops and selling

- **Every position carries a protective stop, entered GTC immediately after the buy.** [E] (Ch 6, "never hold a stock without a protective sell-stop")
- **Use a straight sell-stop (market on trigger), not a stop-limit.** [E] (Ch 6)
- **Never set stops by an arbitrary %** (10–15% rules whipsaw). The stop comes from support and the MA. [E] (Ch 6)

### 5a. Initial stop

**Investor:** [E] (Ch 6, "pay less attention to the MA and more to the prior correction low")
- place it just below the **significant support floor**, which is the prior correction low or the bottom of the base before the breakout;
- pay less attention to the MA when setting the initial stop.

**Trader:** [E] (Ch 6, "almost never pull back more than 4 to 6 percent")
- place it under the **closest prior reaction low**;
- if there is no nearby meaningful low, place it **4–6% below the breakout**, under a round number.

**Round-number rule:** [E] (Ch 6)
- When the stop would land on, or just above, a whole number, move it just below that number. Example: 18 1/8 or 18 becomes 17 7/8.
- Halves act the same way to a lesser degree.
- Trader rule: under $20, every ½ point counts as a round number. Above $20, go under an actual whole number.
- My decimal version: 18.00 or 18.10 becomes 17.88. [I]

**Max risk:** prefer the lowest % risk. Try to keep it **at or below 15%**, with rare exceptions for outstanding charts. If the stop is too far away, find another buy or wait for a safer stop level. [E] (Ch 6, Ch 10)

### 5b. Investor trailing (Stage 2)

1. Give a stock above a rising MA plenty of room. [E] (Ch 6)
2. **Trigger to raise:** a substantial correction of **at least 8–10%**. [E] (Ch 6, "first substantial correction of at least 8 to 10 percent")
   - Do **not** move the stop until the correction has ended and the stock has rallied back toward its prior high. [E]
3. **New level:** [E] (Ch 6, points B, C, E, G, I)
   - just below the **lower of** (a) the correction low and (b) the 30-week MA;
   - adjust under the round number.
   - If the correction low is above the rising MA, the stop goes under the MA.
4. **Keep raising it as the MA rises**, after each correction. [E] (Ch 6, "Continue moving the sell-stop up as the MA advances")
5. **A slight dip below the MA is tolerable** if the MA is still rising and the prior correction low holds. [E] (Ch 6, Merck)
6. **A stop is never lowered.** [I]
   - The book never says it outright, but every worked example only raises stops.
7. **Revisit the stop on weekends.** Only a few adjustments a year are expected. [X] (Ch 6, Skyline)

### 5c. Investor Stage 3 tightening and selling

- **Once the MA stops rising and flattens:** a Stage 3 top is likely forming. Tighten: put the stop just under the latest correction low *even though it is above the MA*. [E] (Ch 6, "more aggressive with your sell-stop")
- **Trivial raises:** skip them, for example 46 to 46½. [X] (Ch 6)
- **Stage 3 profit-taking (Ch 2):** investors take profits on **half** and protect the rest with a stop just below the bottom of the new support. [E] (Ch 2, "take profits on only half of their position")
  - Ch 6 instead just tightens the stop. See Ambiguities.
- **Sell when the stop is hit.** Sell anything in Stage 4, whatever the profit, loss, taxes, yield, P/E, quality or bull market. [E] (Ch 6, Don'ts 1–8)
- **Don't wait for a rally to sell.** [E] (Ch 6, Don't 7)
- **Never average down.** [E] (Ch 6)
- **Never sell a Stage 1 or 2 stock just because it looks "too high" or the P/E is high.** [E] (Ch 6, Ch 10)
- **Laggard rule:** a position lagging badly while the stop is unhit may be lightened and the money moved into a better Stage 2 stock. [E] (Ch 4, "lighten up on that position even if the sell-stop isn't hit")
- **Whipsaw re-entry:** after a stop-out, re-buy if the stock re-breaks out to new heights. [E] (Ch 6, Wal-Mart)
- **Mixed investor/trader:** if a stock skyrockets far above its 30-week MA, lock in ¼ to ½ and trail the rest with an investor stop. [E] (Ch 6)

### 5d. Trader trailing and selling

- **Ignore corrections smaller than 7%.** [E] (Ch 6, "corrections of less than 7 percent")
- **After a correction of at least 7%:** raise the stop to just under that correction low once the stock rallies back near its prior top. [E] (Ch 6)
- **Never hold if price breaks below the 30-week MA, even by a fraction.** [E] (Ch 6, "below the 30-week MA by even a fraction")
- **Tighten further in these cases:** [E/X] (Ch 6, Reynolds; American Capital Management)
  - after a substantial advance, a sideways neutral pattern of about 5 weeks;
  - small tops such as a head-and-shoulders top or a double top.
- **Breakout stalls:** lighten immediately and keep a tight stop about 5% under the breakout, below a round number. [E] (Ch 6, ACA)
- **Price falls back below the breakout point:** traders get out. [E] (Ch 6, "great trades rarely drop back below")
- **Trendline method (a line with at least 3 touches):** [E] (Ch 6)
  - keep a stop for half the position just under the rising trendline and raise it even if price goes sideways;
  - the other half stays under the last correction low;
  - an aggressive trader may use the single trendline stop.
- **Swing-rule target:** [E] (Ch 6, "subtract the next low price from it")
  - target = prior peak A + (A − subsequent low B), applied once price clears A;
  - sell part of the position near the target and let the stop take the rest.

## 6. Short entry

**Gates first:** market (§2b rule 7) and group (§3). Shorts are mostly done in bear markets. [E] (Ch 7)

**Setup (in order):** [E] (Ch 7, "How to Do It Right"; "Taking Some Profitable Steps")
1. A substantial advance over the past year, ideally a steep Stage 2 run with few congestion areas.
   - After a mediocre advance, expect only a minor drop back into the old base.
2. Now in **Stage 3 with a flat, or better a starting-to-decline, MA**, after trending sideways for several weeks.
3. A clear-cut support level, **at or preferably below the MA**, whose break starts Stage 4.

**Breakdown level:** just below the bottom of the support zone, for example 25 7/8 for a 26 floor. [E] (Ch 1)

**RS:** [E] (Ch 7, "Never sell a stock short that has very positive relative strength")
- never short a stock with very positive RS, especially if the RS is trending up;
- above zero is acceptable only if the RS has clearly topped and is trending lower;
- **best** when the RS drops below zero on the breakdown.

**Volume:** not required. A stock can fall of its own weight. [E] (Ch 7)
- More bearish, and preferred for faster declines: a volume rise on the breakdown and a volume decline on the pullback. [E] (Ch 2 footnote, Ch 7)

**Support below:** prefer **little nearby support** under the breakdown. A large trading zone close below slows the decline. [E] (Ch 7, Northwest Airlines vs Hiram Walker)

**Best formation:** a head-and-shoulders top after a dynamic advance. [E] (Ch 7)
- Signs: lighter volume on the head rally; a second decline back to the prior low; a right-shoulder rally failing at or below the head.
- Sell or short when the **neckline breaks**.
- **Don't trust the pattern if the heaviest volume is on the right shoulder.**
- About ⅓ of potential tops break out upward instead, so don't anticipate.
- A bigger top gives a bigger drop. A wider neckline-to-peak swing makes the stock more vulnerable.

**Late Stage 4 continuation short (aggressive / trader):** [E] (Ch 7, "Never Too Late?", Winnebago)
- the stock consolidates *beneath its declining MA* and then breaks down to a new low;
- best in a group that is completely out of favor, and especially in a former institutional favorite;
- there must be resistance overhead for the buy-stop.

**Avoid:** [E] (Ch 7, summary of short-selling don'ts)
- shorting because the P/E is high or the stock has "run too far";
- a stock above its rising 30-week MA (any Stage 2 stock);
- a strong group;
- a **"sucker short"**: a crowded, publicized runner with high short interest. Above roughly 5× average daily volume is dangerous; 3–4× is already high.
- a thin stock: average weekly volume **under 15,000 shares** (1988 figure);
- shorting without a buy-stop.

**My modern version of the thin-stock rule:** I can't check short interest. I will avoid shorts with low dollar volume relative to the universe, and any that are parabolic and widely followed. [I]

**Sizing:** [E] (Ch 7, "When to Sell Short"; quiz answer 5)
- Traders short the entire position on the breakdown.
- Conservative investors short **half** on the breakdown and half on the pullback toward it.
- Pullbacks happen in a bit more than half of cases. About **50% of the best shorts never pull back.**

**Order:** [E] (Ch 7, "Placing the Order"; quiz answer 10)
- a sell-short stop just below support with a limit **at least ½ point below** the stop (more for inactive stocks), GTC;
- the wider cushion existed because of the 1988 uptick rule.

**My decimal version of the short limit:** trigger minus about 2–3%. [I]

**Reward/risk:** [E] (Ch 7)
- Compute the buy-stop first.
- If it must sit **30–40% away**, pass.
- Prefer a 10%-risk short over a 20%-risk one.

## 7. Short stops and covering

- **Always enter a protective buy-stop GTC.** Never be short without one. [E] (Ch 7, "absolutely never go short without first placing")

**Initial buy-stop:** [E] (Ch 7, "right above the prior rally high", "4 to 6 percent")
- **Investor:** just above the prior rally peak inside the top.
- **Trader:** above a nearby prior peak. If the nearest peak is 10–15% away, use 4–6% above the breakdown.
- **Both:** place it *above* any close round number. Example: a 64 7/8 or 64¾ peak becomes 65 1/8. [E]

**Investor trailing:** [E] (Ch 7, "Protecting Your Short with a Buy-Stop")
- While the stock is below a declining 30-week MA, give it room.
- **Trigger:** after the first selloff, wait for a meaningful oversold rally of **at least 8%** (8–10%).
  - Lower the stop only *after* that rally peaks and the stock heads back toward its prior low.
- **New level:** just above the **higher of** the rally peak and the 30-week MA, adjusted above the round number.
- **A slight nudge above a declining MA is OK;** only a significant move above it is a concern.
- **When the MA levels out in a new Stage 1 base:** press the stop to just above the latest rally peak or significant resistance, even if that level is below the MA.

**Trader trailing:** [E] (Ch 7, "Using Buy-Stops the Trader's Way")
- Ignore rallies smaller than 7%.
- After each failed rally, once the stock breaks below the prior low, lower the stop to just above that rally peak.
- **Never stay short if price moves above the 30-week MA, even momentarily.**
- **Trendline:** with a falling trendline of at least 3 touches, keep the stop for half the position just above the trendline and the other half above the last rally peak.

**Swing-rule target:** [E] (Ch 7, "Projecting a Target")
- target = top-area low A − (subsequent high B − A);
- cover half near the target and use the buy-stop for the rest.

**Cover when the buy-stop is hit.** A double bottom or a sideways base usually triggers it. [X] (Ch 7)

**Puts:** an alternative for experienced traders. [E] (Ch 7, Ch 9)
- Buy calls only on Stage 2 stocks and puts only on Stage 4 stocks.
- Allow at least 45–50 days to expiry (ideally 3 months).
- Buy at the money or slightly in the money, with tight stops on the underlying.
- Keep it to a small share of capital.

## 8. Position management

**Number of holdings:** [E] (Ch 4, "Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket")
- one or two stocks is too dangerous; 40–50 is too many;
- with $10–25k, hold **no more than 5–6 stocks**;
- with a six-figure account or more, hold **at most 10–20**.

**Equal dollar amounts per position, not equal share counts.** [E] (Ch 4)

**Spread across groups:** take breakouts from 3–4 strong sectors. [E] (Ch 4)

**Scaling:** [E] (Ch 3, Ch 4)
- investor: ½ on the breakout, ½ on a qualifying pullback;
- trader: all at once.

**Add-ons / averaging up:** [E] (Ch 5, Burlington)
- pros average **up**, never down (Ch 6);
- a second breakout above new resistance on huge volume is a reason to buy more.

**Concentration:** the only explicit "much heavier" weight is the triple-confirmation pattern. [E] (Ch 5)
- My weighting: a full unit for normal A setups, 1.5× for triple confirmation, and no change for continuation entries. [I]

**Treat the portfolio like a fund:** [E] (Ch 6, "tabulate the value of your portfolio")
- tabulate its value **every weekend**;
- a drop of **20–25%** means something is wrong. Either the stops are too wide or a bad climate is whipsawing you, so move to the sidelines.

**Rumor / speculative plays:** keep them to a small percentage of capital. [E] (Ch 5)

**Losers:** [E] (Ch 6)
- a small first loss is the best loss;
- don't take profits on winners to offset losers;
- judge the net result of the whole book.

## 9. Timing details

- **Weekly cycle:** [E] (Ch 3, Ch 4 footnote, Ch 6)
  - do the analysis and the buy list at the weekend;
  - weekly RS and MA are computed off the **Friday close**;
  - between weekends, follow only the short list and current holdings.
- **Orders:** buy-stops, sell-stops, short stops and buy-stops are all resting **GTC** orders. [E]
  - They trigger **intraday** when the level trades. They are not weekly-close rules. [E]
  - They stay until filled or cancelled.
  - A buy-stop entered for a breakout stays open for weeks if needed (the "two or three weeks later" example). [X] (Ch 3)
  - Cancel when the pattern changes.
- **Mental stops:** where real stops aren't allowed (mutual funds, OTC, options), act on a **close** below the level. [E] (Ch 9, "if it closes below your sell-stop point")
- **Stop adjustments:** [E] (Ch 6, Ch 7)
  - investor long: after an 8–10% correction, and only once price rallies back near the prior high;
  - investor short: after a rally of at least 8%, and only once price falls back toward the prior low;
  - trader: on 7% moves, the same sequence.
- **Breakout confirmation is judged the week the stop fills,** using that week's volume and RS, and the following 1–3 weeks. [I]
  - The "sell on first rally if volume poor" rule is checked in the weeks right after the fill. [E] (Ch 4)
- **Pullback-add timing:** typically within weeks of the breakout (Mexico Fund: two weeks). [X] (Ch 3)
  - I allow up to about 8 weeks while price stays above the breakout and the MA. [I]
- **Calendar:** election-cycle and month and day patterns modulate aggressiveness only, never override a chart. [E] (Ch 3, "if a favorable breakout occurs on a Monday, or in May")
- **Shorter MAs belong to other instruments:** daily charts with a 30-day MA for fund trading, a 40-day MA for futures investing. Not used here. [E] (Ch 9)

## Ambiguities and how I will resolve them

1. **Weighted vs simple 30-week MA.** The book defines a simple MA but reads Mansfield's weighted one. **Resolution:** use whatever MA the chart shows. Where the slope is borderline, call it "flat". [I]
2. **When is the MA "rising", "flat" or "declining"?** The book gives no slope threshold. **Resolution:** compare the MA with its value 4–6 weeks earlier. Within about ±1% is flat, above that is rising, below is declining. The visual impression takes precedence. [I]
3. **Breakout trigger on a weekly chart: intraday vs close.** The book's buy-stop fills intraday. The weekly chart shows only high, low and close. **Resolution:** a week whose high exceeds trigger + tick counts as a fill at the trigger, or at the open if the week gapped above it but stayed within my limit. A gap beyond the limit is no fill. Stops work the same way off the weekly low. [I]
4. **"Average volume of the past month / past four weeks."** **Resolution:** compare breakout-week volume with the mean of the prior 4 weeks, excluding the breakout week. For grade A, also require it to exceed every week inside the base. [I, from Ch 4/5 quiz answers]
5. **How far above the breakout is "too late"?** No number is given. **Resolution:** no new entry once price is more than 15% above the trigger, or the stop distance is above 15%. [I]
6. **Investor action at Stage 3: sell half (Ch 2) or just tighten the stop (Ch 6)?** **Resolution:** at the first clear Stage 3 signs, a flat MA plus price crossing it, tighten the stop to just under the latest correction low (Ch 6). If price also closes back below the MA while the MA is flat, sell half (Ch 2). [I]
7. **Which "market" for the gate?** The book uses the Dow. **Resolution:** use the S&P 500 chart as the primary index and the A-D line as the breadth check. [I]
8. **What counts as "the market trend is bullish" when only some gauges agree?** The book uses a majority "weight of the evidence" of about 50 gauges. **Resolution:** use the 3-gauge table in §2d. The S&P stage dominates and the A-D line is the tiebreak. [I]
9. **RS in negative territory but "improving".** **Resolution:** acceptable if the RS line is above its own level of about 13 weeks ago and above its value at the last price peak. Otherwise veto. [I]
10. **Mansfield RS zero line.** Mansfield's zero line is proprietary. **Resolution:** treat the pack's zero line as equivalent; the RS trend matters more than the zero crossing. [I]
11. **Overhead resistance distance.** "A few points overhead" is not a percentage. **Resolution:** a heavy zone, meaning prior support or a multi-touch top, within 20% above the breakout grades the stock B or worse. A zone 20–35% away is acceptable. Nothing in the visible history is A. [I]
12. **Round numbers in modern decimal prices.** **Resolution:** long stops go just below whole dollars, for example x.88 under x.00. Under $20, also go below .50. Short buy-stops go just above whole dollars (x.12). [I]
13. **Trailing stops without a qualifying correction.** If the stock never corrects 8–10%, the investor stop stays at its initial level. **Resolution:** follow the book; no time-based raise. When the MA rises above the initial stop, I still do not move the stop until a correction occurs. [E/I]
14. **Short pullback add:** how close to the breakdown counts as "close"? **Resolution:** within about 5% below the breakdown level, on lighter volume, with price still below the MA. [I]
15. **How the Ch 2 stage quiz handles price below a flat MA after a big decline (Stage 1 vs 4).** **Resolution:** declining MA plus price below it is Stage 4. A flat MA after a prior Stage 4, with price oscillating, is Stage 1. [E, Ch 2 quiz answers]
16. **Short in a mixed market, e.g. the S&P in Stage 3.** The book requires Stage 4 averages for routine shorting. **Resolution:** no shorts until the S&P is below a flat or falling MA, except an A+ head-and-shoulders top as a hedge. [I]
17. **Group stage when the group chart is unclear.** **Resolution:** count the stocks in the group. If most of the individual charts are in Stage 3 or 4, treat the group as unhealthy even if its chart looks borderline. [E/I, Ch 3 cross-check]
18. **Position count and sizing for the backtest account.** **Resolution:** use 10–20 positions, equal dollar amounts, about 5–10% each. The book does not tie position size to stop distance. [E/I]
