    Returns by year vs SPY

    StrategySPYBar under axis = period return below zero

    Return and drawdown are marked to market from the daily NAV. Realised P&L counts only trades closed in the period, so it can disagree in sign with the return. Invested is the average of held positions marked at the adjusted close, as % of NAV; the run caps long exposure at 70% and keeps 30% cash. DD from peak is the deepest point below the all-time high reached during the period. Macro columns count weekly gate readings.

    Pick a trade to see its chart, grade and flags.

    Per-trade flags

    Each card counts the trades that trip one check and the net P&L of those trades. Execution checks compare fills with the day's actual bar. Book checks compare the trade with Weinstein's rules (the spine in weinstein-faithful-core.md). Outcome checks use the post-exit price path. Click a card to list its trades.

    Weinstein conformance (trade audit report)

    Rules are checked over every entry decision, including orders that never filled, so the denominators exceed the 732 trades.

    Post-run validator

    The validator stores at most 10 examples per check. The per-trade flags on this page are computed from the price data, so they list every trade.

    Flat stretches: a year or more below the previous high

    Each row runs from a NAV high to the day NAV first exceeds it again (or to the end of the run). SPY is its total return over the same dates, so the excess is how far ahead of or behind the index the stretch left us.

    Were the picks bad, or the exits? By entry year

    Pick, 8 wk is the stock’s own return from the entry fill to the close 40 trading days later, whatever our stop did; SPY, 8 wk is the index over the same days. A pick year near SPY with a low win rate and many D/F grades lost on exits, not on selection. Medians, so one monster does not dominate.

    Loss cutting: a hard stop at X% below entry

    Replays each trade on its daily bars from the day after entry to the day before the actual exit. If the low reaches entry × (1 − X), the trade exits there (or at the open, if it opened below). Otherwise the actual result stands. Static: freed cash is not redeployed, and nothing can hold longer than it did.

    When do stop exits fill?

    Stop exits with no stop raise: trading days from the first bar whose low reached the initial stop to the exit fill. The book’s stop is a resting order that fills the day the level trades (weinstein-book-reference.md §5.7). At stop is what an intraday fill would have realised: the stop, or that day’s open if it opened below.

    Can D/F trades be avoided at entry?

    The grade is hindsight: it uses the trade’s own result and the 65 trading days after the exit. The question is whether anything known on entry day predicts it. Each row is a cohort by an entry-time field; a useful filter needs a D/F rate well off the overall rate on a cohort with real net P&L at stake.

    Same rules, every salt

    The salt picks which intraday path each daily bar is assumed to take (high first or low first; Bar_shape.seed_for_bar). That moves fills and stop triggers by cents or a day, which changes cash, which changes which queued candidates get funded. The macro trace and the candidate lists are the same.

      How the grades and flags are computed

      Grade (A–F, performance). Taken from the 2026-09-04 yearly review rubric (dev/experiments/yearly-trade-review-2026-09-04/grade.sh), using the 65 trading days after the exit. A: gain ≥ 20%, or any gain with the stock no more than 10% higher afterwards. B: gain under 20%, or a loss where the stock fell another 5% after the exit. C: loss with no big move afterwards. D: loss, then the stock rose ≥ 15%. F: loss, then it rose ≥ 50%. X: exit price under 5% of entry (a delisting stub). A trailing “?” means no bars after the exit.

      Audit grade. The trade audit report's own grade scores the entry setup (stage, RS, volume). Nearly every trade scores A+, so it does not separate good trades from bad ones.

      Stage replay. Weekly stages on the charts and the Stage-4 checks come from stage_chart with the default classifier on adjusted closes. That can differ from the strategy's own reading on a given week.

      Prices. Charts use split-adjusted prices; fill and stop levels are rescaled by each bar's adjusted/raw ratio so they sit on the same scale. Execution checks use the raw bar, with half a percent of slack for rounding; a fill that sits a simple split ratio (2:1, 3:2, 4:3 …) away from the raw bar is on a later split basis, not off the bar, and is not flagged.
